A sensible first year

The aim in year one is competent husbandry, not a honey yield.

Winter before
Join an association, book a beginners' course and find a mentor.
Early spring
Choose one hive system. Prepare the site, stand, frames and a nuc box.
Late spring
Collect a healthy, traceable nucleus and move it into your hive.
First season
Inspect about weekly in swarm season. Learn queen cells. Set a Varroa plan.
Late summer
Take only surplus ripe honey. Assess queen, disease, mites and stores.
Autumn and winter
Finish feeding and mite management. Monitor weight. Review the season.

Five rules that prevent most beginner mistakes

Rule 1
Learn with live bees first
A course and a mentor prevent needless colony losses.
Rule 2
Think ahead of the bees
Swarm boxes, spare frames and feed must exist before they are needed.
Rule 3
Inspect to answer questions
Every opening disturbs the colony and carries some risk.
Rule 4
Make Varroa measurable
Monitor, act when appropriate, then verify the result.
Rule 5
Protect health and food safety
Report suspect disease, use lawful medicines, sell only authentic honey.

Numbers worth knowing

20%
Maximum moisture in honey
The legal limit for ordinary honey. Heather honey may reach 23%.
20 kg
Winter stores for a full colony
A common target of 18 to 22 kg. Large colonies and long winters need more.
7 days
Between inspections in swarm season
Strong colonies need a look about once a week in May and June.
12°C
Before a full spring inspection
A practical minimum, with judgement for wind, sun and the colony.
5 years
Keep medicine records
Honeybees are food-producing animals, so records are a legal duty.
28 days
Register before you sell
Food business registration in England, Wales and Northern Ireland. It is free.

If brood looks seriously wrong, stop.

If you can't confidently explain it, don't move bees, comb, honey or equipment until foulbrood has been ruled out. American and European foulbrood are notifiable. Close the hive and report it.
