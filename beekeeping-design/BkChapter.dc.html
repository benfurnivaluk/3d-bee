Inspections: an end-to-end method

A routine inspection is brief and purposeful. Most don't require finding the queen: fresh eggs show she was laying recently.

The seven inspection questions

Go in to answer these, then close up. Cold, windy or wet weather can chill brood, so adapt how often you open to the season.

QuestionWhat to look forWhat it means
1Queen-right?Eggs, young larvae, normal worker brood, the queen if seenA functioning queen was present recently.
2Brood healthy?White larvae, suitable cappings, a compact patternAbnormal larvae or cappings demand diagnosis.
3Enough stores?Honey, nectar and pollen near brood; hive weightBrood expansion can outpace forage, even in spring.
4Room to lay?Empty polished cells in the brood nestA nectar-bound nest can constrain the queen.
5Room to store?Occupied supers, incoming nectar and the local flowAdd supers before a strong flow congests the hive.
6Swarm preparations?Queen cups, charged cells, sealed cellsRead cell status and queen status together.
7Mites and other threats?Varroa result, deformed bees, wasps, robbing, diseaseRecord it and take planned action.

Opening and closing, step by step

1Plan firstCheck the weather, read your last note and decide the question you must answer. Take spare supers, nuc boxes or frames if you may need them.
2Observe without openingEntrance traffic, pollen loads, fighting, wasps, dead or crawling bees, and whether other hives behave differently.
3PPE on, smoker litCool, steady smoke. Stand out of the main flight line.
4Open gentlyOnly modest smoke. Lift the roof and crownboard carefully, with no bangs or crushed bees.
5Make working spaceRemove an outer frame when safe and rest it in a clean frame holder or box.
6Work frame by frameHold each frame upright over the open brood box and check both faces in good light.
7Check the brood nest carefullyIn swarm season, queen cells hide along side bars and lower edges. Charged cells need a deliberate decision.
8Handle only what you needDon't leave open brood in cold wind or strong sun. Take extra care if the queen is on the frame.
9Rebuild in orderKeep frame spacing, trap no bees under box edges, refit the excluder, close up and secure the hive.
10Record immediatelyDate, weather, queen evidence, brood, stores, space, cells, temper, mite check, actions and the next date.
In peak swarm season, strong colonies often need inspecting about every seven days. That is a guide, not a reason to expose brood in bad weather.

Holding a frame well

  • Hold the lugs firmly but gently.
  • Turn the frame through the vertical. Don't flip a heavy comb flat in bright sun.
  • Keep it above the hive, so a falling queen lands back in the brood box.
  • To see eggs, let sunlight come from behind your shoulder.

What to record

Keep it brief and consistent. 'Eggs and all brood stages?' matters more than 'queen seen?'. Grade food as light, adequate or heavy, record charged cells explicitly, and write one next action with a date.
