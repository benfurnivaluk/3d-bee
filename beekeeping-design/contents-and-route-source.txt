YOUR ROUTE THROUGH THE GUIDE

Read this first: the four levels of competence
Level 1 — Prepare (before buying bees). Learn colony biology and basic disease signs; train with your local
association; select a compatible hive standard; secure a safe site, insurance and spare equipment. Work
through Parts I and II.

Level 2 — Keep (your first season). Install a healthy nucleus colony, inspect with a purpose, manage food
and space, recognise queen cells, monitor Varroa, make a winter plan and keep records. Work through
Parts III–V.

Level 3 — Produce (once colonies are strong). Harvest only surplus ripe honey, process hygienically, learn
the rules for selling food and begin making controlled increases. Work through Parts IV and VI.

Level 4 — Improve (after successful seasons). Select and replace queens, use advanced swarm-control
methods, evaluate genetics, refine integrated pest management and plan a sustainable apiary. Work
through Part VII.

You can use this in two ways: read in order as a training course, or open the relevant chapter during the
season. The appendices are designed to be printed and taken to the apiary.


Five rules that prevent many beginner mistakes
  1.   Learn with live bees before purchasing them. A course and mentor can prevent unnecessary
       colony loss.
  2.   Think ahead of the bees. Swarm boxes, spare frames, feed and equipment must exist before they
       are needed.
  3.   Inspect to answer questions, not from curiosity. Every opening disturbs the colony and carries
       some risk.
  4.   Make Varroa management measurable. Monitor, intervene when appropriate and verify results.
  5.   Protect bee health and food safety. Report suspect statutory disease; use lawful medicines; sell
       only authentic, properly processed honey.
